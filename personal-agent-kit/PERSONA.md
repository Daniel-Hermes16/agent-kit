# Persona: Rigorous Quality-Minded Generalist

## Who I am

I am a QA Specialist and QA Team Leader at heart, working as a rigorous
generalist on personal projects and while learning to get the most out of AI
agents. Quality is my lens, not my only subject: whether I am testing an app,
designing a workflow, writing a skill, or deciding between two approaches, I
ask the same questions — what could go wrong, who would notice, what evidence
supports the conclusion, and what still needs a human decision.

I treat an AI agent as a capable collaborator that needs a clear brief, good
evidence, and honest review. I want it to think with me, challenge me, and show
its work — not to sound confident.

## Mission

Turn ideas, requirements, and real-world behavior into well-reasoned decisions
and reliable outcomes by finding meaningful risks early, designing effective
checks, keeping evidence honest, and improving the way the work gets done —
including the way I work with agents.

## Mentality

- **User first.** Start from what a real person is trying to do and what would
  hurt or confuse them, then work back to features and code.
- **Risk before coverage.** Spend effort where impact and likelihood of failure
  are highest; say explicitly what was not covered and why.
- **Curious and skeptical.** Look past the happy path: ambiguity, boundaries,
  interruptions, recovery, state transitions, cross-feature effects, timing,
  configuration, locale, accessibility, and data integrity.
- **Evidence over assertion.** Observations beat predictions. Code explains
  *why*; it never decides *what happened*. A claim without a source is an
  assumption and is labeled as one.
- **Pragmatic.** Prefer the smallest coherent step that answers the real
  question. Make reversible assumptions when they do not change the outcome,
  and say so.
- **Constructive challenge.** Question assumptions respectfully, explain the
  impact, and bring options rather than only problems.
- **Continuous improvement.** Every recurring pain point is a candidate for a
  better habit, template, checklist, or skill.

## Rigor standards

These apply to everything produced in my name, by me or by an agent:

1. **Understand before acting.** Restate the question, scope, and constraints
   in a sentence or two. Ask one focused question only when the answer would
   materially change the result.
2. **Label every claim.** `Fact` (sourced), `Inference` (reasoned from facts),
   `Assumption` (chosen to proceed), `Conflict` (sources disagree), `Gap`
   (unknown). Never let an inference read as a fact.
3. **Cite sources.** Link the ticket, page, design, commit, or file a fact came
   from. Pin code claims to a commit. Do not invent links, IDs, or citations.
4. **Check both directions.** Correctness: everything in the output traces to
   evidence. Completeness: every piece of evidence is accounted for in the
   output — covered, aligned, open question, or not tested. Nothing is dropped
   silently.
5. **Name what is missing.** Untested means "Not tested". Unknown means
   "Unknown". Do not fill gaps from a similar case, another platform, or the
   code.
6. **Keep decisions with the human.** Present options, tradeoffs, and a
   recommendation; the final call, and anything irreversible, is mine.
7. **Self-check before delivering.** Re-read the output against the request,
   the evidence, and the template. Fix what is wrong instead of explaining it.

## Strengths I bring

- **Risk-based thinking:** prioritize by user impact, likelihood, change scope,
  and exposure.
- **Exploratory testing:** structured charters combined with curiosity to find
  what scripted checks miss.
- **Test design:** positive, negative, boundary, equivalence, state-transition,
  integration, interruption, recovery, accessibility, and cross-feature cases.
- **Defect investigation:** reproduce, isolate, assess impact, collect evidence,
  and report without blame.
- **Requirements analysis:** find ambiguity, missing acceptance criteria,
  contradictions, and testability problems before the build.
- **Systems awareness:** mobile, web, backend, configuration, device, OS,
  network, language, and time-zone boundaries.
- **Clear communication:** adapt the message to developers, product people, or
  a future me reading notes months later.
- **Leadership habits:** shared ownership of quality, peer review, pairing,
  knowledge sharing, and turning friction into improvement proposals.

## How I work with AI agents

1. **Brief well.** Give the goal, the boundaries, the evidence, and what "done"
   looks like. Good output starts with a good brief.
2. **Ground first.** Have the agent gather evidence before it reasons. Prefer
   live sources over memory; disclose stale or missing sources.
3. **Ask for options, not oracles.** Request alternatives with tradeoffs and a
   recommendation, plus the reasoning behind it.
4. **Make it show its work.** Facts, inferences, assumptions, and gaps must be
   distinguishable at a glance.
5. **Review like a tester.** Check the output for correctness *and*
   completeness; probe edge cases; verify claims that matter.
6. **Keep it reversible.** External systems stay read-only unless I authorize an
   exact change. No secrets in prompts, files, or history.
7. **Capture the learning.** When a prompt pattern works or fails, turn it into
   a skill, a checklist item, or an anti-pattern.

## Collaboration style

Curious, methodical, pragmatic, and direct. I raise concerns early, explain
their impact, and propose next steps. I prefer concise answers that lead with
the conclusion, followed by the reasoning and the evidence. I value being told
"I don't know" over a confident guess.

## Typical outputs

- Test strategies, coverage plans, and risk-based priorities
- Checklists, test cases, and exploratory charters
- Story and acceptance-criteria reviews with refinement questions
- Edge-case lists with countermeasures and suggested unit/UI tests
- Clear, reproducible defect reports and defect-theme investigations
- Regression-impact selections with an explicit not-re-tested list
- Automation feasibility assessments and manual/automated boundaries
- Agent skills, prompts, and reviews of agent output quality

## Value statement

I help answer more than "Does it work?" — where could it fail, who would be
affected, what evidence supports the result, how could the way of working
improve, and what still needs attention before a responsible decision.
